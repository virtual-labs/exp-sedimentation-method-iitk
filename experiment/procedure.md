<b>Materials Required:</b><br>
● Andreasen pipette apparatus<br>
● Weighing balance<br>
● Fine powder sample<br>
● Distilled water<br>
● Measuring flask<br>
● Spatula<br>
● Stopwatch<br>
● Oven<br>
● Collection pans or discs<br>

<b>Procedure:</b><br>
● Accurately weigh a known mass of the powder sample and transfer it into a clean measuring flask.
● Add a measured volume of distilled water and shake well to obtain a uniform suspension.<br>
● Insert the Andreasen pipette so that its sampling tip is positioned at a fixed depth below the liquid surface. Start the stopwatch immediately after mixing stops to begin timing sedimentation.<br>
● At fixed time intervals (e.g., 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 minutes), withdraw samples using the pipette and transfer each to a pre-weighed collection pan.<br>
● Place the samples in an oven to remove moisture completely.<br>
● Weigh each dried sample to determine the mass of solids remaining in suspension at each time point.<br>
● Using Stokes’ law, calculate the particle size corresponding to each sampling time. Plot a graph of cumulative percentage of particles finer than each size versus particle diameter to obtain the particle size distribution curve.<br>

<b>Result:</b><br>
The particle size distribution of the given fine powder sample was determined using the sedimentation method with an Andreasen pipette. A plot of cumulative percentage versus particle diameter represents the particle size distribution.<br>

<b>Conclusion:</b><br>
The sedimentation method provides a reliable and simple way to determine particle size distribution for fine powders. It is especially useful for particles within the 0.5–100 μm size range and helps evaluate the uniformity and flow behavior of pharmaceutical powders.
